# Limitations

OpenIntelligence ships on the App Store for iPhone, iPad, and Mac, with paid subscription tiers. It is a real product, and the limits below are the honest boundaries of what it does well — not a disclaimer that it is a prototype.

> **Corrected 2026-08-05.** This document previously opened by calling the app "experimental software... a proof-of-work repository and prototype" and listed "It is not production-ready" and "It is not a commercial product offering" as limits. Both were false: the app has been selling `pro_monthly`, `pro_annual`, and `lifetime_cohort` through StoreKit since v4.4, and `README.md` states it is shipping. A limitations document that understates what the product is undermines the limits that actually matter, which are the safety and technical ones below.

> **Corrected 2026-10-20 against the working tree (`b868fc0` plus the 5.7 work in progress):** the Technical Limits bullet on how much of the window Standard's evidence fills said Deep Think and Maximum use fixed character budgets. From 5.6 Deep Think's chain sizes each session in tokens from the device's window, and the characters are its fallback. The same bullet now also says that Standard's first pass is sized for the Private Cloud Compute window the app assumes when Hybrid can reach it. The answer modes, the agentic loop and its checks are set out in `Docs/CANONICAL_OPENINTELLIGENCE_SOURCE_OF_TRUTH.md`, section 19.

## Product Limits

- It is not a finished enterprise SDK, and `OIEngine` still routes through app-owned services rather than standing alone.
- It is not a sealed binary SDK or a buyer-ready handoff.
- Building from source requires Xcode and Apple-platform development familiarity. Using the shipped app does not.
- Quality modes are not equally proven. Standard has a measured accuracy baseline. Deep Think has been benchmarked, but not in a way that separates it from Standard, and Maximum has no valid figure. See the Technical Limits below. **Corrected 2026-09-29:** from 2026-08-05 this line read "Standard has a measured accuracy baseline; Deep Think and Maximum do not."

## Safety Limits

- It is not validated for regulated workflows.
- It is not intended for clinical, legal, financial, or safety-critical decision-making.
- It is not a clinical decision-support system.
- It is not a diagnostic tool.
- It does not guarantee complete, correct, or exhaustive answers.

## Technical Limits

- Retrieval quality depends on document quality, extraction quality, and query scope.
- OCR, layout parsing, chunking, and entity extraction can fail or lose context.
- Citations indicate supporting sources, not formal proof.
- Confidence signals are engineering aids, not certified reliability metrics.

- **In the one corpus measured, Standard's on-device evidence filled roughly a quarter of the model's window.** Standard reads the window from `SystemLanguageModel.default.contextSize` rather than assuming 4096, but it sizes its evidence in characters, converting its token budget at 1.4 characters per token. A 10,000-character ceiling sits above that conversion and cannot bind at a window of 8192, where the conversion yields at most 9,756 characters; questions the app classes as trivial are held to 2,500 characters. 1.4 is close to what a dense table of part numbers and measurements costs (1.38 measured), and English prose costs far less per character (4.2 to 5.5 characters per token measured). In the Mac benchmark run `sourceonly-after-greedy25` of 2026-09-23 (25 research-paper cases, on-device, window read as 8192 in every case) the evidence sent was 3,177 to 9,494 characters, median 9,324. The assembled prompts were not saved. The same length of each case's recorded chunk text counts as 723 to 2,158 tokens, median 1,993, by the model's own tokenizer, so the token figures are an estimate from like text and the character figures are exact. Structured answer generation was skipped in all 25: that path is gated at 3,600 estimated tokens whatever the window is, and every skip logged an estimate of 4,247 or more. Deep Think and Maximum give their reasoning sessions fixed character budgets that are not derived from the window: 3,500 or 3,000 characters in the reasoning chain, cut to 2,200 once a session carries earlier findings, and 2,500 in Maximum's own loop. Two of their budget helpers do read the window. Not measured: an iPhone or iPad, a corpus of tables, and how much of the window Deep Think and Maximum fill in practice. **Corrected 2026-10-20 against the working tree (`b868fc0` plus the 5.7 work in progress):** the chain's fixed character budgets are now its fallback, not its rule. - **From 5.7, some features read a very large library through a sample, with fixed limits.** The Knowledge Atlas maps at most 6,000 passages and takes its keyword statistics from at most 2,000,000 characters of text spread over the documents. The vocabulary used to expand a question, and the pool that suggested questions are drawn from, are sampled above 4,000 passages. The scan that looks for a stated value in passages retrieval did not return covers every such passage up to 20,000 and an even sample above that, so in a larger library it can miss a value that sits only in a passage outside the sample. Vector and keyword search still read every passage. `[evidence_level: code_verified for the limits; test_verified for the sampling functions (LargeLibraryCostTests, SystemToolsAndScanTests), confidence: high, evidence_source: RAGService.swift visualizationPassageLimit, suggestedQuestionPoolLimit and specScanLimit; QueryEnhancementService.swift vocabularySampleLimit; SQLiteFullTextService.swift statisticsCharacterBudget; Docs/ai/DECISIONS.md 2026-10-10]`
- **From 5.7, one document holds at most 50,000 passages unless it is imported in pieces.** A text, Markdown or JSON Lines file over 8 MB is imported in pieces and is not held to that figure (one finished at 144,247 passages on an iPhone 16 Pro Max on 2026-10-10). Any other file whose text would need more is refused with a message; through 5.6 the chunker stopped at the limit and said nothing. CSV, HTML and Office files are still read whole whatever their size, and a `.json` file is read whole up to 64 MB, as records up to 16 MB and as plain text above that. `[evidence_level: code_verified, confidence: high, evidence_source: SemanticChunker.swift maxChunks; DocumentProcessor.swift importsInPieces and tooManyPassages; Docs/INGESTION_PIPELINE.md]`

## Demo Limits

Demo flows should use generic sample documents. Do not use private credentials, employer material, hospital-specific material, patient data, or sensitive personal data in public examples.
