# Held: the camera's user-facing notes

These notes were written for 5.3 and are **not** in the 5.3 release, because the camera is gated to
Debug builds (`ChatScreen.visionCaptureAction`, `#if os(iOS) && DEBUG`). They are held here rather
than deleted for one reason: the moment that gate comes off, this is the copy that ships, and
rewriting it from the commit log later would lose the plain-language versions of things that took
two days to get right.

They are removed from `WHATS_NEW.md` and `Docs/USER_CHANGELOG.md` because those are read by users
who cannot reach the feature. `Docs/USER_CHANGELOG.md` in particular is copied verbatim into
`OpenIntelligence/Resources/VersionHistory.md` and rendered in Settings, so a camera section there
would have told every 5.3 user about a "Look at Something" button their build does not have.

**When the gate lifts:** move these back under the release that actually carries the camera, not
under 5.3, and change "this is the first release with it switched on" to name that release. Check
every claim against the code again first; several of these describe repairs to a screen that has
still only been used by one person on one phone.

### Your Camera (experimental)
- **Worth saying first: this is an experiment.** The camera screen existed from v1 and was switched off the whole time; this is the first release where it opens at all. What can be tested without a lens is tested; what needs a real camera pointed at a real room has been tried by one person on one phone. Expect it to find things it should not and miss things it should, and judge it as something being tried rather than something finished.
- **Look at Something, in the attachment menu, draws boxes around what it finds and names them.** Text, documents, people, faces, animals and everyday objects are outlined where they are. The screen has been in the app since v1, switched off, and the button used to say "Scan Document", which described one thing it does and set the wrong expectation for the rest.
- **It names objects, not just the room.** Apple's camera intelligence has no single step that finds a thing and tells you what it is: one part finds what stands out but cannot name it, another names a picture but cannot say where anything is. The app runs the first to find each object, cuts that piece out, and runs the second on just that piece. **It finds what stands out, not a list of everything present** — a cluttered counter gives the few things that catch the eye, a blank wall gives nothing.
- **The boxes land on the thing they found, which on the first build with the camera on they did not.** Four reasons, compounding. Nothing had ever told the camera which way up the phone was held, so every frame was analysed sideways and every box came out turned a quarter turn with width and height swapped. The boxes were rebuilt from scratch each update rather than moved, so they blinked rather than followed. The drawing ignored that the preview crops the sides off the picture to fill the screen, pulling every box inward and worst at the edges. And the limit meant to cap analysis at ten times a second sat in code nothing calls, so eight analyses ran on every frame and fell behind the scene. All four are fixed, and the arithmetic is covered by tests. **Where the boxes land on a real scene has still not been checked on hardware.**

- **The camera uses every lens your phone has, so it focuses close up.** It only ever used the main camera before, and anything nearer than that lens can focus stayed blurry. Phones with more than one lens can pass the job to a wider one that focuses closer, which is what macro is, but only if the app asks for all of them together. On a single-camera phone nothing changes.

## From Docs/USER_CHANGELOG.md, the whole section

### Your Camera (experimental)
- **This one is an experiment, and that is worth saying before the rest of it.** The camera screen existed from v1 and was switched off the whole time; this is the first release where anyone can open it. Everything below was built and checked in a matter of days, the parts that can be tested without a camera are tested, and the parts that need a real lens pointed at a real room have been tried by one person on one phone. Expect it to find things it should not and miss things it should. It is off in no sense: you will see the button. But judge it as something being tried rather than something finished, and tell us what it does wrong.
- **Look at Something is in the attachment menu, and the live view draws boxes around what it finds.** Point the camera and text, documents, people, faces, animals and everyday objects are outlined where they are, with a name. The screen has been in the app since v1, switched off. The button used to say "Scan Document", which described one of the things it does and set the wrong expectation for the rest.
- **It names objects now, not just the room.** Before, pointing it at a kitchen gave you three words about the whole picture and no boxes, because Apple's camera intelligence has no single step that finds a thing and tells you what it is: one part finds what stands out but cannot name it, another names a picture but cannot say where anything is. The app now runs the first to find each object, cuts that piece out of the picture, and runs the second on just that piece, so the name belongs to the box it sits on.
- **What it will not do, said plainly:** it finds what stands out, not a list of everything present. A cluttered counter gives you the few things that catch the eye rather than thirty labels, and a blank wall gives you nothing.
- **The boxes now land on the thing they found, which on the first build with the camera on they did not.** There were four separate reasons and they compounded. Nothing in the app had ever told the camera which way up the phone was being held, so every frame was analysed sideways and every box came out turned a quarter turn with its width and height swapped. The boxes were also thrown away and rebuilt from scratch on every update rather than moved, so they blinked on and off instead of following anything. The drawing ignored that the preview crops the sides off the picture to fill the screen, which pulled every box inward, worst at the edges. And the limit meant to stop it analysing more than ten times a second had been written into a piece of code that nothing calls, so it ran eight separate analyses on every single frame and fell behind whatever it was looking at. All four are fixed.
- **The camera view stopped burying what you were pointing it at.** Once the boxes landed in the right places it became obvious the rest was in the way. Every box used to carry its own name tag, so a page of text put one tag per line stacked on top of each other, right over the thing you were reading; text boxes no longer carry one, because the box shows you the line and the Live Text panel below already shows you the words. Every tag also read "50%", which is what the text recogniser reports for almost everything, so that number is gone from the places it meant nothing. The panel of counters and sliders that covered nearly half the screen is now off until you tap the chart button for it. And the scene labels no longer print on top of each other.
- **It keeps up better.** Working out where a body's arms and legs are is the most expensive thing the camera does and it finds nothing at all when there is no person in shot, which is most of the time. It now only looks when something person-shaped or animal-shaped was there a moment ago.
- **It uses all your phone's lenses now, so it focuses close up.** Before, it only ever used the main camera, and anything nearer than that camera can focus simply stayed blurry. Phones with more than one lens can hand the job to a wider one that focuses closer, which is what people mean by macro, and that only happens if the app asks for all the lenses together rather than picking one. It now does. On a phone with a single camera nothing changes.
- **The skeletons move smoothly instead of smearing.** What looked like motion blur was a blur: every joint was drawn as a soft blurred dot, and because the app threw the whole skeleton away and rebuilt it ten times a second, those dots appeared in new places rather than travelling to them. Joints are crisp rings now, and each one glides to its next position over exactly the gap between readings, so the skeleton is drawn continuously rather than in steps.
- **The wireframe robot is gone.** A cartoon body outline was drawn over people: a head, shoulders, a waist and two legs, all at fixed proportions of the box around them. It was the same figure every time no matter how the person was standing, because nothing measured them. The real skeleton was already being drawn next to it from actual body tracking, so two outlines described the same person and disagreed. The cartoon face on detected faces went for the same reason, since nothing was ever measuring where eyes and mouths are.

- **Still not tried on real hardware by us.** The arithmetic behind where a box goes is covered by tests now, which is the part that can be checked without a phone. Whether they land correctly on a real scene is the part that cannot. Tell us what you see.
