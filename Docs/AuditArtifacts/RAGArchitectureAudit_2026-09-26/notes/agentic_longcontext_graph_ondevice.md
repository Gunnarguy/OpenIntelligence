# Agentic retrieval, long context vs RAG, graph/tree retrieval, and small-model / on-device RAG: published evidence 2024-2026 (current as of 2026-09-26)

Tagging used on every finding: **[primary, ...]** = read by me in a fetched primary source (repo README by the paper authors, Apple developer documentation, Microsoft Research pages, verified by raw text). **[secondary, ...]** = fetched first-party engineering blog or guide. **[inferred (search summary), ...]** = figure or claim known only from a web-search summary; the URL is the page the summary drew from, not a page I read. arxiv.org, huggingface.co, openreview.net, aclanthology.org, research.google, trychroma.com, databricks.com and machinelearning.apple.com were blocked for fetching, so most paper numbers are search-summary level unless a README repeats them. Items first published before 2023 are flagged "(pre-2023; possibly superseded)". Descriptions of OpenIntelligence's pipeline (Deep Think / Maximum fixed chain, extractive shortcut, RAPTOR-lite) come from the brief and were not verified by me.

## 1. Agentic RAG: letting the model decide when and what to retrieve (gains, cost, small-model viability, failure modes, schema-constrained tool calls)

### Takeaway
Iterating retrieval helps multi-hop and compositional questions, and 3-8B models *trained* to search (RL or distillation, 2025-2026) beat fixed single-shot RAG by large margins on Wikipedia-style QA. Prompted self-triggering methods (FLARE, released Self-RAG) give little or negative benefit with 7-8B generators in an independent uniform reproduction, and the newest sub-4B evidence says small models should be made to search consistently rather than left to decide. Most of the benefit comes from about three searches. The documented failure modes are loops, over-search and under-search, guessing from parametric memory, and collapse on multi-turn tool calls. Constrained decoding fixes malformed calls but not wrong ones.

### Cited Findings

**Original prompted and adaptive methods**
- ReAct (Yao et al., arXiv Oct 2022, ICLR 2023; pre-2023, possibly superseded). The HotpotQA error analysis names a ReAct-specific failure: the model "repetitively generates the previous thoughts and actions" (a loop), classed as a reasoning error. ReAct's hallucination false-positive rate in success mode was 6%. Exact failure-category percentages were not verified. — [inferred (search summary), ReAct arXiv 2210.03629](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03629)
- Self-Ask (Press et al., arXiv Oct 2022, EMNLP Findings 2023; pre-2023, possibly superseded). The "compositionality gap" (a model answers every sub-question but fails the composed question) stays at about 40% across GPT-3 sizes. Self-ask's explicit follow-up questions let a search engine answer the sub-questions, which improves accuracy. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2210.03350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.03350)
- IRCoT (Trivedi et al., arXiv Dec 2022, ACL 2023; pre-2023, possibly superseded). Interleaving retrieval with chain-of-thought steps improved retrieval by up to 21 points and downstream QA by up to 15 points on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultihopQA, MuSiQue and IIRC with GPT-3. "Similar substantial gains" held out of distribution and with much smaller Flan-T5-large without additional training. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2212.10509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10509)
- FLARE (Jiang et al., May 2023, EMNLP 2023) retrieves when a tentative next sentence contains low-probability tokens. On 2WikiMultihopQA FLARE-direct scored 51.0 EM, against 39.4 for single-time retrieval and 28.2 for no retrieval. It nearly tied on ASQA (41.3 vs 40.0) and WikiAsp (UniEval 53.4 vs 52.4). It sets no retrieval budget, so per-query cost is unbounded. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2305.06983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.06983); [inferred (search summary), beancount.io research log, May 2026](https://beancount.io/bean-labs/research-logs/2026/05/18/flare-active-retrieval-augmented-generation)
- Self-RAG (Asai et al., Oct 2023, ICLR 2024) retrieves on demand ("can retrieve multiple times or completely skip retrieval") and critiques its own output through reflection tokens. A Critic and a Generator are trained with next-token prediction on reflection-token data generated by GPT-4. Released 7B and 13B Llama-2 models. — [primary, Self-RAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AkariAsai/self-rag/main/README.md). The paper reports beating ChatGPT and retrieval-augmented Llama2-chat on PopQA, TriviaQA, PubHealth, ARC-Challenge, Biography and ASQA, with higher citation precision and recall on ASQA. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2310.11511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11511)
- CRAG (Yan et al., Jan 2024). A lightweight retrieval evaluator returns a confidence that triggers correct, incorrect or ambiguous actions, with web search as a fallback and "decompose-then-recompose" filtering of retrieved documents. — [primary, CRAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HuskyInSalt/CRAG/main/README.md). Reported gains over standard RAG: PopQA +19.0 accuracy, Biography +14.9 FactScore, PubHealth +36.6, ARC-Challenge +8.1. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2401.15884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15884). An independent open-source reproduction (Mar 2026) scored 54.4% on PopQA against the original 54.9%. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2603.16169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16169)
- Adaptive-RAG (Jeong et al., Mar 2024, NAACL 2024). A smaller LM (T5-Large) is trained on automatically collected labels to classify query complexity and route between no retrieval, single-step and multi-step retrieval. — [primary, Adaptive-RAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/starsuzi/Adaptive-RAG/main/README.md). With FLAN-T5-XL it reached F1 46.94 at 2.17 average steps against 4.69 steps for always-multi-step. With GPT-3.5 it reached F1 50.91 at 1.03 steps against 3.0. — [inferred (search summary), alphaXiv overview of arXiv 2403.14403](https://www.alphaxiv.org/overview/2403.14403)

**Reinforcement-learned search agents (2025)**
- Search-R1 (Mar 2025) trains interleaved reasoning and search-engine calls with RL (PPO, GRPO), supports Llama3 and Qwen2.5, and runs against local sparse/dense retrievers or online search. — [primary, Search-R1 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PeterGriffinJin/Search-R1/main/README.md). Reported gains over RAG baselines across seven QA datasets: +41% for Qwen2.5-7B and +20% for Qwen2.5-3B. An earlier version reported 26% / 21% / 10% for Qwen2.5-7B / Qwen2.5-3B / LLaMA3.2-3B. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2503.09516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09516)
- Search-R1 follow-up empirical study (May 2025). Format rewards improve results while intermediate retrieval rewards have limited impact. Starting from a general-purpose LLM is more stable than starting from a reasoning LLM. The choice of search engine shapes training dynamics and robustness at inference. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2505.15117](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.15117)
- R1-Searcher (Mar 2025) uses two-stage outcome-reward RL: first a format/retrieve reward to learn to invoke search, then an F1 answer reward. Models are Qwen-2.5-7B-Base and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct. The authors state that "the capability of the Base LLMs largely influences whether the model can directly start training from Zero" and claim their 7B approaches GPT-4o-mini. — [primary, R1-Searcher README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RUCAIBox/R1-Searcher/main/README.md)
- ReSearch (Mar 2025), succeeded by ReCall (Apr 2025), trains tool-call reasoning with RL "without requiring any supervised data on tool use trajectories". Its example uses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. — [primary, ReCall README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Agent-RL/ReCall/main/README.md)
- DeepResearcher (Apr 2025) is a 7B agent trained with RL in the real web. It gained up to 28.9 points over prompt-engineering baselines and up to 7.2 over RAG-based RL agents. Emergent behaviours include planning, cross-validation and "maintain honesty when unable to find definitive answers". — [primary, DeepResearcher README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GAIR-NLP/DeepResearcher/main/README.md)
- ZeroSearch (May 2025) trains 3B policies against an LLM that simulates a search engine and "outperforms real search engine-based models while incurring zero API cost". — [primary, ZeroSearch README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Alibaba-NLP/ZeroSearch/main/README.md)
- s3 (May 2025) trains only the searcher and leaves the generator as a black box, needing "a fraction of the data used by prior methods". — [primary, s3 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pat-jj/s3/main/README.md)
- Search-o1 (Jan 2025, EMNLP 2025) adds agentic search to reasoning models plus a "Reason-in-Documents" module that digests noisy retrieved documents before they enter the reasoning chain. — [primary, Search-o1 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/sunnynexus/Search-o1/main/README.md)

**Small models (under 4B) as search agents**
- Agent Distillation (May 2025, NeurIPS 2025) distils Qwen2.5-32B agent trajectories with retrieval and code tools into small models. The 0.5B, 1.5B and 3B agents match 1.5B, 3B and 7B CoT-distilled models respectively, and the 7B agent beats the 32B CoT model. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2505.17612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17612). The teacher model and the released distilled 1.5B agent are confirmed in the repo. — [primary, agent-distillation README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Nardien/agent-distillation/main/README.md)
- "Search, Do not Guess" (Apr 2026):
  - Small models under 4B "invoke search tools less frequently and are more prone to hallucinations" on multi-hop tasks. The paper calls this parametric hallucination.
  - Naive distillation from LLM trajectories gives marginal gains, because the trajectories rely on parametric knowledge the small model lacks.
  - "Adaptive search strategies in SLMs often degrade performance."
  - An Always-Search Policy fine-tune improves on agent distillation by +17.3 on Bamboogle and +15.3 on HotpotQA.

  Single source. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2604.04651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04651)
- "Search Wisely" (May 2025) defines over-search and under-search. One model "could have avoided searching in 27.7% of its search steps". β-GRPO gives a 3B model +4% average EM with 1.21% fewer over-searches and 7.33% fewer under-searches. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2505.17281](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17281)

**Cost, calls and latency**
- Budget-Constrained Agentic Search (Mar 2026, LREC 2026) tested six LLMs on three QA benchmarks:
  - Accuracy rises with search count "until roughly three searches, after which returns plateau".
  - Hybrid lexical+dense retrieval with lightweight re-ranking gave the largest average gain in the ablation grid.
  - Larger completion budgets help mainly on multi-document synthesis.

  — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2603.08877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08877)
- "Fishing for Answers" (Sep 2025) ran 1,000 questions over 40,000 government documents. Token-budgeted one-shot retrieval (for example a 32K budget with rule-based metadata filtering) scored 91.0, iterative reasoning-agent retrieval 90.0, and fixed top-k RAG 81.0. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2509.04820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04820)
- Anthropic (Jun 13 2025, frontier models only):
  - Agents use about 4× the tokens of chat, and multi-agent systems about 15×.
  - Token usage alone explained 80% of performance variance on BrowseComp.
  - A multi-agent setup beat single-agent Claude Opus 4 by 90.2% on an internal research eval, especially on breadth-first queries.

  — [secondary, Anthropic "How we built our multi-agent research system"](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)
- Anthropic (Dec 19 2024; the page notes tooling has changed since) distinguishes workflows ("LLMs and tools are orchestrated through predefined code paths") from agents (LLMs "dynamically direct their own processes and tool usage"). It recommends "finding the simplest solution possible", notes that agentic systems "often trade latency and cost for better task performance", and says workflows "offer predictability and consistency for well-defined tasks". — [secondary, Anthropic "Building effective agents"](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)
- Practitioner claims (weak evidence, unverified):
  - An agentic loop makes 3-8 LLM calls per query.
  - It costs about 10× naive RAG.
  - Latency is about 500 ms for naive RAG against 3-4 s agentic.

  — [inferred (search summary), dev.to practitioner post](https://dev.to/sapotacorp/agentic-rag-what-it-actually-costs-versus-what-it-actually-delivers-3975)

**Documented failure modes**
- **Loops.** ReAct repeats earlier thoughts and actions (above). Apple's own tool-calling documentation warns that with `ToolCallingMode.required` "you must define an exit condition ... otherwise, the model continues to call the tool". — [primary, Apple "Expanding generation with tool calling"](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/expanding-generation-with-tool-calling). LightRAG warns that "some models (locally deployed Qwen models in particular) occasionally fall into an endless-output loop" during extraction. — [primary, LightRAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HKUDS/LightRAG/main/README.md)
- **Over-search, under-search and guessing.** See "Search Wisely" and "Search, Do not Guess" above.
- **Self-triggering signals that never fire.** FLARE matches no-retrieval in FlashRAG's Llama3-8B reproduction (above). The cause is not stated there.
- **Router failures at the confidence extremes.** Self-Route routes too early when retrieved chunks look superficially relevant but the question needs distributed evidence. It escalates unnecessarily when the evidence is a negative-premise check or a keyword-local fact. This analysis appears to come from the 2026 Pre-Route paper. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2605.10235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10235)
- **Ignoring or over-trusting retrieved evidence.** See section 2 (small-model context disregard, ClashEval).

**Structured and schema-constrained tool calls**
- Apple guided generation "uses constrained sampling ... Constrained sampling prevents the model from producing malformed output". — [primary, Apple "Generating Swift data structures with guided generation"](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/generating-swift-data-structures-with-guided-generation)
- Tool calling in Apple's framework:
  - By default the model decides whether to call a tool.
  - `ToolCallingMode` offers `.allowed` (default), `.required` (the model must call one or more tools before responding) and `.disallowed`.
  - The model "can call a tool multiple times in parallel".
  - The OS version that introduced `ToolCallingMode` is not stated in the passage I read.

  — [primary, Apple tool-calling article](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/expanding-generation-with-tool-calling)
- Apple TN3193 (first published 2025-10-06, updated 2026-03-31) on tools in a small context:
  - Tool definitions (name, description, parameters), tool outputs and Generable schemas all consume the 4,096-token window.
  - "Give the model a maximum of 3–5 tools to choose from."
  - "In the cases where the model should always have information from a tool, run the tool directly before you call the model and integrate the tool's output to the prompt directly."
  - When arguments must come from the model, "generate those in one session, then run your tool using normal programming, then have the model process the tool's output in a new, second session".
  - "RAG can be used as a tool call, or as a step you run before calling the on-device foundation model."

  — [primary, Apple TN3193](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3193-managing-the-on-device-foundation-model-s-context-window)
- Constrained decoding in small LLMs (Sep 2026):
  - Schema validity rose from 78.6-92.9% to 100% for every model.
  - It rescued type-coercion failures (for example prices emitted as strings) but gave "no benefit" for instruction-semantic failures such as multi-step function calling.
  - A constrained 1B model exceeded an unconstrained 3B model.
  - The "cheapest reliable configuration" was XGrammar with a 3-4B model.

  — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2609.23742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23742)
- "The Constraint Tax" (May 2026): constraints help when they remove syntax failures and hurt when "they convert visible format failures into valid wrong decisions". — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2605.26128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.26128)
- "Let Me Speak Freely?" (Aug 2024, EMNLP 2024 Industry): format restrictions, especially JSON-mode constrained decoding, degraded reasoning while improving classification accuracy. Looser formats reduced variance on reasoning tasks. A secondary summary gives a "10–15%" reasoning drop, which I have not verified. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2408.02442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.02442)
- Small models on the Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard (TinyLLM study, Nov 2025):

  | Model | Overall | Multi-turn |
  |---|---|---|
  | Qwen3-1.7B | 55.49% | 16.88% |
  | xLAM-2-1b | 53.97% | 8.38% |
  | Qwen3-0.6B | 45.76% | 1.38% |

  Models under 1B fail multi-turn tasks. For top models, paraphrasing the user query cut AST accuracy by 11-19% absolute, and adding related distractor tools cost another 1-8%. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2511.22138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22138)

### Inferences
- Running a fixed chain from code is not in itself behind practice. Both Anthropic's workflow guidance and Apple's TN3193 favour code-controlled steps for well-defined tasks, and running retrieval directly when its output is always needed. The 2023-2026 evidence adds three things a fixed chain over pre-computed windows of **one** retrieval lacks:
  - A second retrieval round conditioned on what the first found (IRCoT; FlashRAG multi-hop gains; the budgeted-search plateau near 3 searches).
  - A retrieval-quality or sufficiency check that can trigger re-retrieval or abstention (CRAG's evaluator; Sufficient Context in section 2).
  - An explicit budget.
- The configuration the newest small-model evidence warns against is giving a roughly 3B model open-ended autonomy over *whether* to search: under-search and guessing, multi-turn tool-call collapse, Apple's documented looping in required mode, and tool schemas eating a 4K window. The pattern consistent with the evidence is: code guarantees retrieval happens, and the small model at most proposes sub-questions or rewrites in a separate short session, which TN3193 itself describes.
- The strongest small-model agent results (3-7B via RL or distillation) all require weight training. For Apple's system model the only supported route is a LoRA adapter (section 2), so "better agentic behaviour" on device is a training and maintenance decision, not a prompt change.
- The cross-table comparisons above are confounded by different base models (Llama2-7B vs Llama3-8B vs Qwen2.5). FlashRAG is the only within-table apples-to-apples view, and there, at 8B, IRCoT and Adaptive-RAG give modest multi-hop gains while FLARE and released Self-RAG do not.

### Gaps
- I found no published evaluation of agentic retrieval with Apple's on-device model (any OS version). Every small-agent result uses Qwen or Llama models on Wikipedia-style QA, not personal document libraries.
- No phone-measured latency for multi-step agentic retrieval loops was found; the cost evidence is server token counts.
- ReAct's per-category error percentages, and the exact IRCoT, FLARE and Self-RAG tables, could not be read (arXiv and ACL Anthology were blocked).
- Whether iOS 27's "better at ... calling your custom tools" translates into measured multi-turn reliability is not publicly benchmarked, as far as I found.

## 2. Small models using retrieved context: faithfulness, utilization, training/adapters, measured on-device latency and energy

### Takeaway
Sub-8B readers are the weak link. They often ignore context or are knocked off correct answers by it, hallucinate rather than abstain when context is insufficient, and fail at the structured extraction that graph RAG needs. Training for RAG (distractor-aware fine-tuning, refusal and citation alignment, LoRA robustness adapters) produces the largest measured improvements. Apple supports LoRA adapters, but each is locked to one system-model version. On-device RAG research targets index memory and prompt-token reduction, and per-token energy on phones measures about 3× worse than batched server inference.

### Cited Findings

**How small models use context**
- "Can Small Language Models Use What They Retrieve?" (Mar 2026) tested models from 360M to 8B:
  - Even under oracle retrieval, models "fail to extract the correct answer 85–100% of the time", and the dominant failure is "complete disregard of the provided context".
  - Adding retrieval context "overturns 42–64% of answers the model previously answered correctly", an effect driven by the presence of context rather than its quality.
  - Robust utilization "appears to require substantially more than 7B parameters".

  Single source. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2603.11513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11513)
  - **Partial disagreement:** in FlashRAG's reproduction, an 8B reader gained from standard RAG on NQ (22.6→35.1) and PopQA (21.7→36.7) but lost on 2Wiki (33.9→21.0) and WebQA (18.8→15.7). — [primary, FlashRAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RUC-NLPIR/FlashRAG/main/README.md)
- MiniRAG (Jan 2025, HKUDS, the same group as LightRAG). SLMs suffer "severe performance degradation" in existing RAG frameworks. On the authors' on-device benchmark LiHua-World (one year of a virtual user's chat records; single-hop, multi-hop and summary questions), accuracy / error:

  | Reader | NaiveRAG | LightRAG | MiniRAG | GraphRAG |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Phi-3.5-mini | 41.22% / 23.20% | 39.81% / 25.39% | 53.29% / 23.35% | "/" (no effective responses) |
  | Qwen2.5-3B | 43.73% | 39.18% | 48.75% | "/" |
  | MiniCPM3-4B | 43.42% | 35.42% | 51.25% | "/" |
  | gpt-4o-mini | 46.55% | **56.90%** | 54.08% | 35.27% |

  On MultiHop-RAG with Phi-3.5-mini: NaiveRAG 42.72%, LightRAG 27.03%, MiniRAG 49.96%. MiniRAG claims performance "comparable to LLM-based methods even when using SLMs while requiring only 25% of the storage space". — [primary, MiniRAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HKUDS/MiniRAG/main/README.md)
- Sufficient Context (Nov 2024, ICLR 2025):
  - An autorater (Gemini 1.5 Pro, 1-shot CoT) judged context sufficiency with 93% accuracy and 0.94 F1 on 115 human-labelled multi-hop questions.
  - "Many 'gold' snippets are in fact insufficient, and longer context windows beyond 6k tokens yield negligible gains."
  - With sufficient context, GPT-4o was 83% correct, 13% hallucinated and 4% abstained; Claude 3.5 Sonnet was 86% / 3% / 11%.
  - With insufficient context, hallucinations climb to 15-40%.
  - Selective generation (sufficiency × self-rated confidence) lifts selective accuracy by 2-10 points at the same coverage.

  — [primary, sufficientcontext README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hljoren/sufficientcontext/main/README.md). Smaller models (Mistral 3, Gemma 2) "hallucinate or abstain often, even with sufficient context". Gemma's incorrect-answer rate rises from 10.2% with no context to 66.1% with insufficient context. — [inferred (search summary), Google Research blog on sufficient context](https://research.google/blog/deeper-insights-into-retrieval-augmented-generation-the-role-of-sufficient-context/)
- ClashEval (Apr 2024, NeurIPS 2024 D&B) used more than 1,200 questions in six domains, including drug dosages and records, with perturbed evidence. LLMs "adopt incorrect retrieved content, overriding their own correct prior knowledge over 60% of the time". — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2404.10198](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10198)
- FaithEval (Oct 2024, ICLR 2025) has 4.9K problems covering unanswerable, inconsistent and counterfactual contexts. "Larger models do not necessarily exhibit improved faithfulness." — [inferred (search summary), FaithEval repo page](https://github.com/SalesforceAIResearch/FaithEval)

**Training and adapter approaches**
- RAFT (Mar 2024) trains with distractor documents and chain-of-thought answers that cite the oracle document. On Llama2-7B it gained +35.25% over Llama2-7B+RAG on HotpotQA and +76.35% on Torch Hub. Keeping some training examples without the gold document improved robustness. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2403.10131](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10131)
- Ret-Robust (Llama2-13B with a trained LoRA for robustness to irrelevant context) is the strongest method in FlashRAG's uniform table. It scored NQ 42.9 and PopQA 57.2 against standard RAG's 35.1 and 36.7, although its base model differs. — [primary, FlashRAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RUC-NLPIR/FlashRAG/main/README.md)
- The released Self-RAG 7B underperformed Llama3-8B standard RAG on TriviaQA (38.2 vs 58.9) and HotpotQA (29.6 vs 35.3) in FlashRAG. This is confounded by the base models (Llama2-7B vs Llama3-8B). — [primary, FlashRAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RUC-NLPIR/FlashRAG/main/README.md)
- Trust-Align (Sep 2024, ICLR 2025 oral) defines Trust-Score as response correctness, attribution quality, and "Refusal Groundedness: ability of the model to discern if the question can be answered given the documents". "Various prompting methods, such as in-context learning, fail to adapt LLMs effectively to the RAG task." Aligned LLaMA-3-8b gains +10.7 on ASQA, +29.2 on QAMPARI and +14.9 on ELI5 over open models of comparable size. — [primary, trust-align README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/declare-lab/trust-align/main/README.md). It was applied to LLaMA 1B-8B, Qwen-2.5 0.5B-7B and Phi3.5 (3.8B), and 26 of 27 aligned models beat baselines. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2409.11242](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11242)
- Apple adapters. "The system model uses ... LoRA"; each adapter takes "approximately 160 MB"; "Each adapter is compatible with a single specific system model version ... you will need to train a different adapter for every version of the system model"; adapters "have steep requirements to train (and re-train for OS updates)". Before considering adapters, Apple says to "try to get the most out of the system model using prompt engineering or tool calling". Reasons it gives for adapters: subject-matter expertise, adhering to a style, format or policy, prompting "isn't achieving the required accuracy or consistency", and lower latency. Deployment needs the Foundation Models Framework Adapter Entitlement, and the adapter is hosted on a server (Background Assets). Training needs a Mac with Apple silicon and at least 32 GB, or a Linux GPU machine. — [primary, Apple Foundation Models adapter training page](https://developer.apple.com/apple-intelligence/foundation-models-adapter/)
- Apple's 2025 tech report describes the on-device model as about 3B parameters with 2-bit quantization-aware training and KV-cache sharing: 37.5% of layers reuse the KV cache of the first block. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2507.13575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.13575)

**Measured latency, memory and energy on devices**
- Apple (June 2024 model, iPhone 15 Pro): "time-to-first-token latency of about 0.6 millisecond per prompt token, and a generation rate of 30 tokens per second", before token speculation. — [inferred (search summary), Apple ML Research "Introducing Apple's On-Device and Server Foundation Models"](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-apple-foundation-models); corroborated by [inferred (search summary), Trail of Bits analysis Jun 2024](https://blog.trailofbits.com/2024/06/14/understanding-apples-on-device-and-server-foundations-model-release/). This is a 2024 model and device; the iOS 27 model and newer phones differ.
- MobileRAG (Jul 2025) ran on a Galaxy S24 with Qwen2.5-0.5B/1.5B and DeepSeek-R1-1.5B:
  - EcoVector keeps only cluster centroids and an HNSW graph over them in RAM, with clusters on disk; search is 1.72-8.89× faster.
  - Selective Content Reduction re-chunks retrieved documents and keeps only the most similar pieces, cutting prompt tokens by up to 42% "without degrading accuracy".
  - Time to first token improved 1.18-1.41×.
  - Retrieval-phase power fell 61-75% against IVF-DISK and EdgeRAG, and power fell 28.2-40.2% against an "Advanced RAG" baseline.

  — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2507.01079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01079)
- EdgeRAG (Dec 2024) ran on a Jetson Orin Nano with 8 GB shared memory. It prunes embeddings inside clusters, generates embeddings on demand and caches them. Retrieval and generation quality stayed within 5% of IVF and flat indexes on BEIR, with large tail-latency and memory reductions. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2412.21023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21023)
- "The Battery Price of edge AI" (Sep 2026) measured 18 configurations on a Pixel 8, an iPhone 14 and an A100 server. On-device inference is "on average 3 times less energy-efficient than batched server inference". Energy against quantization bit-width is non-monotonic. Eight configurations lie on the accuracy/energy Pareto front, which the authors say enables "battery-aware model routers". — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2609.11940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11940)

### Inferences
- The evidence says a roughly 3B reader should get fewer, higher-precision passages and an explicit abstention path, not "more context to be safe". Utilization failures, flips caused by context, and insufficient-context hallucination all get worse as irrelevant text is added.
- For a 3B system model, a RAFT or Trust-Align style LoRA adapter (distractor robustness, refusal groundedness, citations) is the evidence-backed lever. Its cost is an adapter per system-model version (about 160 MB each), a training dataset, and an evaluation process. Prompting alone "fail[s] to adapt LLMs effectively to the RAG task" according to Trust-Align.
- Structured extraction, as graph RAG needs, is where small readers fail hardest (MiniRAG's "/" cells; see also section 4).
- Energy per token is higher on device than on a batched server, but the app's privacy constraint (only the final answer may go to PCC, after consent) limits using the server for intermediate steps. That trade-off is a product decision the evidence informs but does not settle.

### Gaps
- There is no independent evaluation of Apple's on-device model (2025 or iOS 27) for context faithfulness, abstention or distractor robustness.
- I found no measured energy per token for Apple's Foundation Models framework; the Battery Price study used open models on an iPhone 14.
- A "0.1 J per token per billion parameters" rule of thumb appeared in a search summary without a traceable primary source, so I omitted it.
- MiniRAG's numbers are self-reported on the authors' own benchmark, and I found no independent replication.

## 3. Long context versus RAG: when a 32K window beats tighter retrieval, when it hurts, and routing between a 4K local and a 32K server model

### Takeaway
With strong frontier models and enough budget, long context wins on average (Self-Route, 2024; Li et al., 2025). RAG with good ordering matches or beats long context at a fraction of the tokens (OP-RAG), and benchmark after benchmark concludes there is no universal winner (LaRA). Effective context is far shorter than claimed once literal matching is removed: 4B-8B models fall below 50% of their short-context accuracy by 2K-8K tokens on NoLiMa (Gemma 3 4B by 2K, GPT-4.1 Nano and Gemma 3 12B by 4K, Llama 3.1 8B by 8K). Adding passages helps up to a point, then hurts because of hard negatives. Routing by answerability or by a cheap classifier recovers most of long context's quality at much lower cost.

### Cited Findings
- Self-Route (Li et al., Jul 2024, EMNLP 2024 Industry):
  - "When resourced sufficiently, LC consistently outperforms RAG in terms of average performance. However, RAG's significantly lower cost remains a distinct advantage."
  - Self-Route routes by model self-reflection on whether the retrieved chunks can answer the question.
  - It stays within 0.2-2.2% of full long context while cutting token cost by up to 65% (Gemini-1.5-Pro) and 39% (GPT-4o).
  - Over 50% of queries (up to 81.74% for Gemini-1.5-Pro) are resolved at the cheap RAG-and-route step.

  — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2407.16833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16833); [inferred (search summary), Liner review of the paper](https://liner.com/review/retrieval-augmented-generation-or-longcontext-llms-comprehensive-study-and-hybrid)
- OP-RAG (Yu et al., NVIDIA, Sep 2024) keeps retrieved chunks in their original document order. Quality against chunk count forms an inverted U with "sweet points". On Llama3.1-70B:
  - En.QA: OP-RAG with 16K retrieved tokens scored 44.43 F1, against 34.26 for long context without RAG at about 117K tokens.
  - En.MC: 84.72 accuracy with 16K tokens.

  — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2409.01666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01666); [inferred (search summary), SuperAnnotate blog](https://www.superannotate.com/blog/rag-vs-long-context-llms)
- "Long-Context LLMs Meet RAG" (Oct 2024, ICLR 2025): quality "initially improves first, but then subsequently declines" as more passages are retrieved. The cause is hard negatives, and stronger retrievers (e5) produce more damaging ones than BM25. Fixes: retrieval reordering (training-free), RAG-specific fine-tuning, and fine-tuning with intermediate reasoning. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2410.05983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05983)
- LaRA (Feb 2025, ICML 2025) has 2,326 cases at 32K and 128K across novels, papers and financial reports, with locate, compare, reason and hallucination-detection tasks. "There is no definitive conclusion regarding the superiority of RAG versus LC LLMs." The outcome depends on context length, context type, the model's long-context ability and the task type. — [primary, LaRA README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Alibaba-NLP/LaRA/main/README.md). Parameter size and retrieved-chunk characteristics also matter. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2502.09977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09977)
- "Long Context vs. RAG: An Evaluation and Revisits" (Dec 2024 / Jan 2025): "LC generally outperforms RAG in question-answering". — [primary, LC_VS_RAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lixinze777/LC_VS_RAG/main/README.md). Summarization-based retrieval performs comparably to long context, while chunk-based retrieval lags. RAG does better on dialogue-based and general queries. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2501.01880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01880)
- Databricks Mosaic study (Aug 2024 blog, Nov 2024 arXiv) ran 20 LLMs from 2K to 128K (2M where possible). Every model improved from 2K to 4K, many kept improving to 16-32K, and "only a handful" stayed consistent above 64K. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2411.03538](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03538)
- NoLiMa (Feb 2025, ICML 2025; README updated Jul 2025) builds needle questions with minimal lexical overlap, so models must "infer latent associations". "At 32K ... 10 models drop below 50% of their strong short-length baselines. Even GPT-4o ... [drops] from ... 99.3% to 69.7%." Effective length (the longest context keeping 85% of base score) and selected scores:

  | Model | Effective length | Base | 4K | 32K |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | GPT-4.1 | 16K | 97.0 | 91.7 | 79.8 |
  | GPT-4o | 8K | 99.3 | 95.7 | 69.7 |
  | Llama 3.1 8B | 1K | 76.7 | 44.1 | 14.2 |
  | Gemma 3 4B | under 1K | 73.6 | 16.4 | 0.9 |
  | GPT-4.1 Nano | under 1K | 80.7 | 36.7 | 9.4 |

  On NoLiMa-Hard at 32K: o3 58.5, Gemini 2.5 Pro 58.6, Llama 3.3 70B with CoT 10.1. — [primary, NoLiMa README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adobe-research/NoLiMa/main/README.md)
- RULER (Apr 2024; README updated with 2025 models) uses literal-match synthetic tasks, with the threshold set at Llama2-7B's 4K score of 85.6. Small models score much longer effective lengths here:

  | Model | Effective length | 4K | 32K | 128K |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Phi3-mini 3.8B | 32K | 92.2 | 87.5 | 66.7 |
  | Qwen3-4B | 64K (see note) | 95.1 | 87.8 | — |
  | Llama3.1-8B | 32K | 95.5 | 87.4 | — |
  | EXAONE-4.0-1.2B | 32K | — | — | — |

  — [primary, RULER README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NVIDIA/RULER/main/README.md). **Disagreement with NoLiMa**, which gives under 1K to 1K for the same class of models. Note: the README lists Qwen3-4B (and Qwen3-8B) as 64K effective although their 64K scores (77.8 and 82.1) are below the 85.6 threshold, a possible README inconsistency. Usable context depends heavily on whether the answer shares words with the question.
- Lost in the Middle (Jul 2023, TACL 2024) shows U-shaped accuracy by position. GPT-3.5-Turbo loses more than 20% when the relevant document moves from the start to the middle of 20 documents. In the worst case, 20- and 30-document settings score below closed-book accuracy (56.1%). — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2307.03172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03172); [inferred (search summary), Arize paper reading](https://arize.com/blog/lost-in-the-middle-how-language-models-use-long-contexts-paper-reading/)
- Chroma "Context Rot" (Jul 2025) tested 18 models, including GPT-4.1, Claude 4, Gemini 2.5 and Qwen3. Every one degraded as input grew, even on trivial repeat and retrieve tasks. Lower needle-question similarity degraded faster and distractors compounded the loss. Claude models tended to abstain and GPT models to hallucinate. — [inferred (search summary), Chroma research page](https://www.trychroma.com/research/context-rot); [inferred (search summary), ZenML summary](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/context-rot-evaluating-llm-performance-degradation-with-increasing-input-tokens)
- Sufficient Context: "longer context windows beyond 6k tokens yield negligible gains". — [primary, sufficientcontext README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hljoren/sufficientcontext/main/README.md)
- BenchmarkQED (Microsoft, Jun 5 2025). With every method "limited to the same 8k tokens for answer generation", "increasing Vector RAG's context window from 8k to 120k tokens did not improve its performance compared to LazyGraphRAG". Against a 1M-token vector RAG on GPT-4.1, LazyGraphRAG still won every comparison, and missed significance only on relevance for DataLocal queries. — [primary, Microsoft Research BenchmarkQED blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/benchmarkqed-automated-benchmarking-of-rag-systems/)
- LongBench v2 (Dec 2024) has 503 multiple-choice questions over 8K-2M-word contexts. Human experts reached 53.7% under a 15-minute limit. The best model answering directly reached 50.1%, and o1-preview "with longer reasoning" reached 57.7%. — [primary, LongBench README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/THUDM/LongBench/main/README.md)
- Inference scaling for long-context RAG (Oct 2024, ICLR 2025): standard RAG plateaus around 10^4 tokens, while demonstration-based RAG (DRAG) and iterative DRAG (IterDRAG) improve near-linearly with effective context. DRAG is best at 16K-32K and IterDRAG at 128K and above. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2410.04343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.04343)

**Routing between a small and a large model, or between RAG and long context**
- Hybrid LLM (Microsoft, ICLR 2024) routes each query to a small (edge-deployable) or large model by predicted difficulty and a quality level that can be tuned at test time. It makes "up to 40% fewer calls to the large model, with no drop in response quality". — [primary, Microsoft Research publication page](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/hybrid-llm-cost-efficient-and-quality-aware-query-routing/)
- RouteLLM (LMSYS, 2024) trained routers that "reduce costs by up to 85% while maintaining 95% GPT-4 performance" on MT Bench. — [primary, RouteLLM README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lm-sys/RouteLLM/main/README.md)
- Pre-Route (May 2026) routes between RAG and long context by reasoning over lightweight document metadata (type, length, first snippet) before retrieving. It beats Always-RAG, Always-LC and Self-Route on cost-effectiveness on LaRA and LongBench-v2, and the routing ability distils to smaller models. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2605.10235](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10235)
- ConsRoute (Mar 2026) routes across cloud, edge and device tiers using a reranker's judgment of cross-tier answer consistency, and reuses prefill hidden states as the on-device query representation to avoid extra encoders. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2603.21237](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.21237)
- Apple's two tiers (WWDC26 session 319):
  - "The on-device model offers 4k, and with PCC you get 32K. And the PCC model supports reasoning."
  - PCC "requires an internet connection" and "offers a daily limit per user", while the on-device model "has no request limits".
  - "Getting structured output with Generable, or calling Tools, works just the same with the PCC model".
  - The on-device model is now "better at instruction following and calling your custom tools".
  - PCC is available to apps with fewer than 2M downloads, at no token cost to the developer.

  — [primary, WWDC26 session 319 page](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/319/)

### Inferences
- The 32K server window is most defensible for evidence spread across many passages or documents (multi-document synthesis, the reduce step of map-reduce, verification). Filling it with more loosely ranked chunks is not supported: OP-RAG's inverted U, hard negatives, Sufficient Context's "beyond 6k negligible", and NoLiMa's 32K degradation even for GPT-4o-class models.
- For the 4K local model, NoLiMa's result that 4-8B models lose half their accuracy by 2K-8K on non-literal questions (Gemma 3 4B by 2K, Llama 3.1 8B by 8K; at 4K Gemma 3 4B keeps 16.4 of 73.6 and Llama 3.1 8B 44.1 of 76.7) matters more than RULER's optimistic 32K. A lease-notice question whose wording differs from the document ("notice period" vs "days prior written notice") is exactly the non-literal case.
- An answerability self-check (Self-Route), a metadata or TF-IDF router (Pre-Route; RAGRouter-Bench in section 5) and a difficulty router (Hybrid LLM) are all evidence-backed ways to decide when to escalate from 4K to 32K. On Apple's platform escalation is also bounded by per-user daily limits, network availability, and this app's consent rule.

### Gaps
- No study evaluates Apple's PCC model or on-device model on NoLiMa, RULER, LaRA or LongBench. How effective the 32K window is on PCC is unknown.
- The Self-Route and OP-RAG figures were not verified in the papers (arXiv blocked). They rest on two secondary summaries each.
- No study found compares routing between a 4K and a 32K model of the *same family* with privacy constraints on what may be escalated.

## 4. Graph- and tree-structured retrieval (GraphRAG, LightRAG, HippoRAG 2, RAPTOR): gains by query type, indexing cost with small summarizers, failure modes

### Takeaway
Graph and tree indexes win on global and thematic questions and on multi-hop association, and tie or lose on single-hop factoid lookups. Several structure-heavy methods cost 5-10 F1 on simple QA. Most headline wins are LLM-judge pairwise preferences with documented position and length bias. Building the index needs a capable model: 7B-class and smaller models fail or degrade at entity and relation extraction, and graph methods run with SLMs underperform naive RAG. The 2024-2026 trend is toward cheap or deferred structure (LazyGraphRAG, LinearRAG, HippoRAG 2), with the graph used as a handle back to source text rather than a replacement for it.

### Cited Findings

**Microsoft GraphRAG and successors (primary)**
- The repo is "largely in maintenance mode" because "since our first release in July 2024 the capabilities of frontier models have changed dramatically". It warns "GraphRAG indexing can be an expensive operation ... start small", and "strongly recommend[s]" prompt tuning because out-of-the-box results may not be best. — [primary, GraphRAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/graphrag/main/README.md)
- GraphRAG is intended for questions "whose answers span many documents, as well as thematic questions such as 'What are the top themes in this dataset?'". It "depends on well-constructed indexing prompts", indexing is "relatively expensive", and it works best on "natural-language text data focused on an overall topic or theme and rich in identifiable entities". — [primary, GraphRAG RAI_TRANSPARENCY.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/microsoft/graphrag/main/RAI_TRANSPARENCY.md)
- The original paper (Apr 2024) reports substantial comprehensiveness and diversity gains over naive RAG for "global sensemaking questions over datasets in the 1 million token range", with root-level community summaries at a fraction of the token cost. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2404.16130](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16130)
- Dynamic community selection (Nov 15 2024). Static global search map-reduces over every community report at a fixed level and is "expensive and inefficient because it includes many lower-level reports that are not informative to the user query". A GPT-4o-mini relevance rater "achieved a very similar retrieval rate as a GPT-4o rater" at a fraction of the cost. Average cost fell 77% against static search at level 1. At level 3 dynamic search cost 34% more on average but won 58.8% on comprehensiveness and 60.0% on empowerment. — [primary, Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/graphrag-improving-global-search-via-dynamic-community-selection/)
- LazyGraphRAG (Nov 25 2024):
  - Indexing costs "identical to vector RAG and 0.1% of the costs of full GraphRAG".
  - Global queries: quality comparable to GraphRAG Global Search at "more than 700 times lower query cost".
  - At 4% of GraphRAG global query cost it "significantly outperforms all competing methods" on local and global queries.
  - Evaluation: 5,590 AP news articles, 100 synthetic queries (50 local, 50 global), LLM-judged comprehensiveness, diversity and empowerment.
  - A low-cost LLM runs the relevance tests.

  Read via WebFetch extraction. — [primary, Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazygraphrag-setting-a-new-standard-for-quality-and-cost/)
- BenchmarkQED (Jun 5 2025) classifies queries as local or global and as data-driven or activity-driven. LazyGraphRAG with GPT-4o "won all 96 comparisons, with all but one reaching statistical significance". "Competing methods performed relatively better on the query classes for which they were designed: GraphRAG Global for global queries, Vector RAG for local queries." One configuration used GPT-4o mini throughout. — [primary, Microsoft Research BenchmarkQED blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/benchmarkqed-automated-benchmarking-of-rag-systems/)

**LightRAG, MiniRAG, HippoRAG 2, RAPTOR**
- LightRAG (Oct 2024; README current in 2026) reports LLM-judged win rates over NaiveRAG ("Overall") of 67.6% agriculture, 61.2% CS, 84.8% legal and 60.0% mixed. Against GraphRAG it wins 54.8%, 52.0% and 52.8%, and loses 49.6% on the mixed set. — [primary, LightRAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HKUDS/LightRAG/main/README.md)
- LightRAG's own model requirements: "LightRAG has higher capability requirements for Large Language Models (LLMs) than traditional RAG because it requires LLMs to perform complex entity-relation extraction". "For local deployment, Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct is a reasonable minimum" for extraction. The query model "should be *stronger* than the extraction model". — [primary, LightRAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HKUDS/LightRAG/main/README.md)
- LightRAG's paper claims GraphRAG global retrieval needs about 610,000 tokens per query, against under 100 tokens and one API call for LightRAG. It also says GraphRAG must regenerate community reports on incremental updates (about 1,399 × 2 × 5,000 tokens in its example). — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2410.05779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05779)
- MiniRAG with SLMs: GraphRAG could not produce effective answers and LightRAG fell below naive RAG (section 2 table). — [primary, MiniRAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HKUDS/MiniRAG/main/README.md)
- HippoRAG 2 (Feb 2025, ICML 2025) "improves associativity (multi-hop retrieval) and sense-making ... without sacrificing their performance on simpler tasks", using "significantly fewer resources for offline indexing compared to ... GraphRAG, RAPTOR, and LightRAG". — [primary, HippoRAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/OSU-NLP-Group/HippoRAG/main/README.md). Specific numbers:
  - Average F1 59.8 against NV-Embed-v2's 57.0.
  - About +7 F1 on associative tasks.
  - NQ F1 63.3 against 61.9.
  - Structure-based methods (RAPTOR, GraphRAG, LightRAG) "generally reduce performance on simple QA by 5–10 F1".
  - About 9M against 115M indexing tokens on MuSiQue.

  — [inferred (search summary), Emergent Mind summary of arXiv 2502.14802](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/hipporag-2)
- RAPTOR (Jan 2024, ICLR 2024) builds a tree of recursive cluster summaries. With GPT-4 it scored 90.3% on QuALITY, "20% absolute" over the previous best. With GPT-4 it reached QASPER F1 55.7 (+2.7 over DPR); NarrativeQA ROUGE-L was 30.87 against 29.26 for SBERT alone. In its analysis 4% of 150 sampled summary nodes (built with gpt-3.5-turbo) contained hallucinations, mostly minor added information or wrong extrapolation, and summaries compress children by about 72%. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2401.18059](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.18059)
- SVD-RAG (Jul 2026) is an LLM-free tree alternative. It comes within 1-5% of RAPTOR's retrieval quality while building the tree 317× faster. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2607.10316](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.10316v1)

**Independent comparisons and evaluation critiques**
- "RAG vs. GraphRAG: A Systematic Evaluation" (Feb 2025, KDD 2026): RAG is better on single-hop and detail-oriented questions and GraphRAG on multi-hop reasoning. Community-based global search "can sacrifice query-specific details". For query-based summarization, standard RAG stayed closer to the source while community GraphRAG "often produced overly abstract summaries". — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2502.11371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11371)
- GraphRAG-Bench ("When to use Graphs in RAG", Jun 2025, ICLR 2026) notes that "recent studies report that GraphRAG frequently underperforms vanilla RAG on many real-world tasks". It evaluates four levels: fact retrieval, complex reasoning, contextual summarization and creative generation. — [primary, GraphRAG-Bench README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GraphRAG-Bench/GraphRAG-Benchmark/main/README.md). Its conclusion is that basic RAG suffices for simple fact retrieval and graphs help complex reasoning. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2506.05690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.05690)
- "How Significant Are the Real Performance Gains? An Unbiased Evaluation Framework for GraphRAG" (Jun 2025) finds position, length and trial bias in LLM-judge GraphRAG comparisons. A 25-token length gap can swing win rates by more than 50%. On ROUGE-2 against gold answers GraphRAG loses to plain RAG: 6.99 vs 10.08 on SQuALITY and 3.23 vs 6.32 on QMSum. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2506.06331](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06331)
- "Do We Still Need GraphRAG?" (Apr 2026): dense RAG already does well on general QA, where GraphRAG adds +0.47 on average. GraphRAG adds +27.23 on multi-hop QA. Agentic search "partially narrow[s] the gap" for dense RAG. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2604.09666](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09666)
- EffiRAG, "When Is Graph Structure Worth Its Cost?" (Sep 16 2026): graph structure is worth its cost "only when it serves to locate original source evidence rather than to generate summaries or reasoning chains". EffiRAG was preferred to LightRAG-hybrid on 93 of 120 queries at 57% lower system cost. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2609.18099](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18099)
- "GraphRAG on Consumer Hardware" (May 2026) ran Microsoft GraphRAG with Llama 3.1 8B, Mistral 7B, Qwen 2.5 7B and Phi-4-mini 3.8B on an 8 GB GPU. Phi-4-mini "fails to complete the pipeline due to structured-output errors". Qwen 2.5 extracted the fewest entities (330) yet gave the best answers (3.3/5); Llama 3.1 extracted about 3.5× as many (1,172) and scored 2.7/5. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2605.20815](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20815)
- MMGraphRAG (Jul 2025, multimodal): using a 7B graph-construction model with a 72B answer model gave 60.2% overall accuracy. The model used for graph construction mattered more than the model used for answering. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2507.20804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.20804)
- LinearRAG (Oct 2025, ICLR 2026) builds a relation-free "Tri-Graph" with lightweight entity extraction and semantic linking. It "eliminates LLM token costs during graph construction" and scales linearly with corpus size. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2510.10114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10114)

### Inferences
- For a personal document library, the evidence supports tree or graph summaries for global and thematic questions ("what do my documents say about X overall"). It does not support them as the answer source for factoid lookups such as a notice period or a weight. Those should come from original chunks, with any summary layer used at most as a pointer back to source text (EffiRAG; BenchmarkQED's DataLocal result; the 5-10 F1 simple-QA penalty).
- A summary layer written by a roughly 3B model is higher-risk than RAPTOR's published setup, which used gpt-3.5-turbo summaries and GPT-4 answers and still had 4% hallucinated nodes. MiniRAG, the consumer-hardware GraphRAG study and LightRAG's 30B-class extraction minimum all point the same way. Summary nodes therefore need a source link so a claim can be checked.
- The cheap-structure line of work (LazyGraphRAG, LinearRAG, SVD-RAG, HippoRAG 2) is the 2025-2026 direction and fits on-device limits better than GraphRAG-style LLM extraction at ingestion.
- LLM-judge win rates such as LightRAG's and LazyGraphRAG's should be read against the documented judge biases and ROUGE reversals. Gold-answer accuracy on the app's own question types is the missing evidence.

### Gaps
- No study measures RAPTOR or GraphRAG summary quality with a roughly 3B summarizer on personal documents. The closest are MiniRAG (chat logs, self-reported) and the consumer-hardware GraphRAG study (EHR schemas, 7-8B).
- The HippoRAG 2 and RAPTOR numbers come from secondary summaries (arXiv blocked).
- Indexing energy or time on a phone for any graph or tree method was not found.

## 5. Query routing and question-type handling: learned vs rule-based routing, expected-answer-type detection as a gate

### Takeaway
Routing is well supported: a small trained classifier (Adaptive-RAG), even TF-IDF+SVM (RAGRouter-Bench), gets most of the quality of always-expensive pipelines at far lower cost, and no single RAG paradigm wins across query types. Expected-answer-type classification is an old, established QA technique (Li & Roth 2002; Watson's type coercion 2012; both pre-2023, possibly superseded as methods). Modern RAG work gates answers on *sufficiency* and answerability instead. I found no 2024-2026 paper that evaluates an answer-type gate (duration, money, date, quantity) in LLM RAG, so that design rests on older QA evidence plus the modern abstention results.

### Cited Findings
- Adaptive-RAG: a T5-Large complexity classifier (no retrieval, single-step, multi-step) trained on automatically derived labels (section 1). — [primary, Adaptive-RAG README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/starsuzi/Adaptive-RAG/main/README.md)
- RAGRouter-Bench (Feb 2026) has 7,727 queries and 21,460 documents in four domains, each query labelled factual, reasoning or summarization, across five RAG paradigms. "No one-size-fits-all paradigm exists across query-corpus pairs", and "token consumption spans over an order of magnitude across paradigms". — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2602.00296](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00296)
- Lightweight routing baselines on RAGRouter-Bench (Apr 2026). The best was TF-IDF + SVM: macro-F1 0.928, accuracy 93.2%, and 28.1% simulated token savings against always using the most expensive paradigm. Lexical TF-IDF beat MiniLM sentence embeddings by 3.1 macro-F1. Medical queries were hardest to route and legal queries easiest. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2604.03455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03455)
- Self-Route (answerability self-reflection) and Pre-Route (metadata-based structured reasoning) are LLM-based routers (section 3). Hybrid LLM and RouteLLM are small/large model routers (section 3).
- Li & Roth "Learning Question Classifiers" (COLING 2002; pre-2023, possibly superseded as a method) defines a two-level answer-type taxonomy with six coarse classes, including NUMERIC, and fine classes, trained on the 5,500-question UIUC/TREC set. — [inferred (search summary), ACL Anthology C02-1150](https://aclanthology.org/C02-1150.pdf). I recall the NUMERIC fine classes as including date, period, money, weight, distance and count, but did not verify them in a fetched source.
- IBM Watson DeepQA (2011-2012; pre-2023, possibly superseded) detects a lexical answer type (LAT) in the question and scores each candidate's type consistency with it through type coercion (TyCor). The score feeds answer ranking rather than rejecting candidates outright. "Multi-source multi-strategy" soft TyCor "significantly outperforms" a single-source hard type filter. — [inferred (search summary), "A Comparison of Hard Filters and Soft Evidence for Answer Typing in Watson" (ISWC 2012)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-35173-0_16)
- Sufficient Context selective generation abstains on a combination of an insufficiency signal and self-rated confidence, adding 2-10 points of selective accuracy (section 2). — [primary, sufficientcontext README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hljoren/sufficientcontext/main/README.md)
- UAEval4RAG (Dec 2024, ACL 2025) covers six unanswerability categories, including out-of-database and false-presupposition questions. Handling of unanswerable queries "is neither uniformly good nor predictable from their answerable accuracy". Configurations that maximize correctness on one dataset "can have the lowest acceptance rate on unanswerable queries". — [inferred (search summary), ACL Anthology 2025.acl-long.415](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.415/)
- RefusalBench (Oct 2025, EACL 2026) is a generative evaluation of selective refusal in grounded models. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2510.10390](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.10390)
- Trust-Align treats "Refusal Groundedness" as one of three trust axes and trains for it (section 2). — [primary, trust-align README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/declare-lab/trust-align/main/README.md)

### Inferences
- The "1 lb" answer to a lease-notice (duration) question is the textbook case that answer typing was built to catch: the question's expected type is a period, and the candidate's type is weight. Watson's result argues for scoring type consistency rather than hard-filtering inside a ranker. For a *pre-model extractive shortcut* that answers without the model, though, a type mismatch has no downstream ranker to absorb it, so a hard gate or a fall-through to the model is the conservative reading. That last point is my inference; no paper tests it.
- A modern equivalent of the gate is a sufficiency or answerability check before showing any answer (Sufficient Context, UAEval4RAG, Trust-Align), whether the model or a pattern extractor produced it. UAEval4RAG's finding that answerable accuracy does not predict unanswerable behaviour means the extractive path needs its own unanswerable-query tests.
- Rule-based lexical routing is not outdated. TF-IDF+SVM beat sentence embeddings for query-type routing in 2026, so a cheap on-device classifier is an evidence-backed first stage.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024-2026 LLM-RAG paper that measures an expected-answer-type gate (duration, money, date, quantity), or unit-consistency checks, as a precision filter. Evidence is limited to pre-LLM QA systems and generic abstention work.
- Li & Roth's fine-class list was not verified in a fetched source (cogcomp and ACL Anthology unreachable).

## 6. Map-reduce or iterative summarization for whole-library questions under a small context window

### Takeaway
Divide-and-conquer over chunks is the documented way to answer corpus-level questions with a small window. Apple's TN3193 explicitly recommends splitting work across fresh sessions, and a 4B model with a structured map-reduce protocol beat 70B long-context models on InfiniteBench (LLM×MapReduce). The known costs:
- Hierarchical merging is more coherent but can amplify hallucinations ("summaries of summaries") unless intermediate steps are re-grounded in source text.
- Incremental updating keeps more detail but is less coherent.
- Full map-reduce over every summary is expensive; relevance-pruned selection with a small rater cuts cost by 77% or more.
- Even strong systems score far below humans on corpus-level summarization with citations (SummHay).

### Cited Findings
- Apple TN3193. For tasks needing more context, "split the task into smaller steps, run each step with a new language model session, and then assemble the results together". For a long article: summarize chunks "with a new session, combining the results together, and then repeating this process", "adding the result of the previous summarization to the prompt so it conveys the contextual information". — [primary, Apple TN3193](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3193-managing-the-on-device-foundation-model-s-context-window)
- LLM×MapReduce V1 (Oct 2024) is training-free and splits a document into chunks. A "structured information protocol" carries crucial information from map to reduce, and "in-context confidence calibration" resolves conflicts between chunks. On InfiniteBench, **MiniCPM3-4B × MapReduce** averaged 59.29% (excluding Zh.QA), against Qwen2-70B 51.92%, Kimi-Chat 52.96% and GPT-4 55.33% as reported by InfiniteBench. On En.QA it scored 28.39 against GPT-4's 22.44; on Code.Debug it was weaker (25.63 vs 54.31). V2 (Apr 2025) adds entropy-driven convolutional test-time scaling for long-to-long generation. — [primary, LLMxMapReduce V1 README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/thunlp/LLMxMapReduce/main/LLMxMapReduce_V1/README.md); [primary, LLMxMapReduce README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/thunlp/LLMxMapReduce/main/README.md)
- Chain of Agents (Google, Jun 2024, NeurIPS 2024) has worker agents read segments sequentially and pass notes forward, then a manager synthesizes. It improves "by up to 10%" over RAG, full-context and multi-agent baselines on QA, summarization and code completion, and "mitigates long context focus issues by assigning each agent a short context". — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2406.02818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.02818)
- Speculative RAG (Google, Jul 2024): a small specialist model drafts answers in parallel from distinct subsets of retrieved documents, and a larger generalist model verifies them. Accuracy rises "by up to 12.97%" and latency falls by about 51% on PubHealth. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2407.08223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08223)
- BooookScore (Oct 2023, ICLR 2024) found hierarchical merging more coherent than incremental updating, while incremental updating kept more detail, which human raters valued. The "83% preference" figure in one summary is unverified. Omissions of entities and events dominate both methods. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2310.00785](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.00785)
- Context-Aware Hierarchical Merging (Feb 2025, ACL Findings 2025): recursive merging "can amplify LLM hallucinations". Replacing or refining intermediate summaries with relevant source passages, or aligning them to the source through citations, consistently beat plain hierarchical merging for Llama 3.1 models on legal and narrative data. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2502.00977](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00977)
- Summary of a Haystack (Jul 2024, EMNLP 2024) uses 10 haystacks of about 100K tokens and 92 query-focused summarization tasks, scored on insight coverage plus citation (Joint Score). The benchmark evaluates long-context models ("Full") and RAG pipelines with several retrievers, including an oracle. — [primary, SummHay README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/salesforce/summary-of-a-haystack/master/README.md). Even with oracle document relevance, systems trail the human estimate (56%) by more than 10 points, and long-context GPT-4o and Claude 3 Opus score below 20% without a retriever. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2407.01370](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01370)
- GraphRAG global search map-reduces over community reports, and dynamic selection with a GPT-4o-mini rater cut cost 77% (section 4). LazyGraphRAG defers summarization to query time and indexes at vector-RAG cost (section 4). — [primary, Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/graphrag-improving-global-search-via-dynamic-community-selection/); [primary, Microsoft Research blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/lazygraphrag-setting-a-new-standard-for-quality-and-cost/)
- Li et al. (2025): summarization-based retrieval performs comparably to long context, while chunk retrieval lags. — [inferred (search summary), arXiv 2501.01880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01880)

### Inferences
- A multi-session chain over windows is the pattern Apple recommends for a 4K model, and published map-reduce systems (LLM×MapReduce with a 4B model, Chain of Agents) show it can beat single long-context passes. The "several sessions over windows" shape of Deep Think / Maximum therefore matches documented practice for whole-document and whole-library questions. The evidence-backed refinements are:
  - Structured intermediate outputs with confidence, so the reduce step can resolve conflicts (LLM×MapReduce).
  - Re-grounding intermediate summaries in source passages or citations (context-aware merging).
  - Relevance-pruning which windows get a map call, using the cheap model as rater (dynamic community selection).
  - Using this machinery for global questions, not for factoid lookups, where a single well-ranked passage suffices.
- The reduce or verification step is where a 32K PCC model adds the most, given its window and reasoning. Speculative RAG's small-drafter / large-verifier split is the published template. Whether the app's consent rule allows intermediate map outputs to be sent to PCC decides whether this is available.

### Gaps
- No map-reduce study uses a model constrained to 4K per call on device. LLM×MapReduce's chunk size for MiniCPM3-4B was not checked.
- BooookScore's detail-preference percentage and SummHay's per-system numbers were not verified in the papers.

## 7. Implications for an app with a 4K on-device model and a 32K server model

### Takeaway
The evidence does not condemn a code-orchestrated multi-session pipeline. Apple recommends exactly that for a 4K model, and published map-reduce and chain designs beat single long passes. It does indicate four gaps:
- Single-retrieval designs cannot recover from a retrieval miss.
- Small models should not be left to decide whether to retrieve.
- Every answer path, including non-model extractive shortcuts, needs a sufficiency or answer-type gate before display.
- Summary and graph layers help global questions but hurt or add nothing on factoid lookups.

### Cited Findings (each implication restates evidence from sections 1-6)
1. **Keep retrieval under code control. Add a second, conditioned retrieval round and a budget; do not delegate the decision to search to the 3B model.**
   - Supporting evidence: TN3193's advice to run tools directly and split argument generation from processing ([primary](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3193-managing-the-on-device-foundation-model-s-context-window)); Anthropic's workflow guidance ([secondary](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)); the plateau at about 3 searches ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08877)); IRCoT and Adaptive-RAG multi-hop gains at 8B ([primary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RUC-NLPIR/FlashRAG/main/README.md)).
   - The case against letting the small model decide: under-search, and adaptive search degrading SLMs ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04651)); multi-turn tool calling collapsing in 1-2B models ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22138)); Apple's warning that required tool mode loops without an exit condition ([primary](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/foundationmodels/expanding-generation-with-tool-calling)).
2. **Gate every displayed answer on sufficiency and on answer-type consistency, including answers the regex/extractive shortcut produces.**
   - Supporting evidence: selective generation adds 2-10 points of selective accuracy ([primary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hljoren/sufficientcontext/main/README.md)); unanswerable handling is not predicted by answerable accuracy ([inferred](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.415/)); Watson-era type-consistency scoring (pre-2023) ([inferred](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-35173-0_16)); small models hallucinate on insufficient context ([inferred](https://research.google/blog/deeper-insights-into-retrieval-augmented-generation-the-role-of-sufficient-context/)).
   - No modern paper tests an answer-type gate specifically (section 5 gap).
3. **Feed the 4K model few, well-ranked, order-preserved passages, and expect distractors to hurt.**
   - Supporting evidence: 4-8B models are below 50% of baseline by 2K-8K tokens on non-literal questions ([primary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adobe-research/NoLiMa/main/README.md)); hard negatives ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05983)); context flips small-model answers ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11513)); OP-RAG ordering ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.01666)); prompt reduction by up to 42% without accuracy loss on a phone ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01079)).
   - Tool schemas and Generable schemas come out of the same 4,096 tokens ([primary](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3193-managing-the-on-device-foundation-model-s-context-window)).
4. **Use the 32K PCC model for evidence spread across documents, for reduce and verification steps, and for questions a cheap router or an answerability check flags, not as a place to pour more chunks.**
   - Supporting evidence: long-context gains mostly stop by 16-32K ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03538)); negligible gains beyond 6K for sufficiency ([primary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/hljoren/sufficientcontext/main/README.md)); GPT-4o-class models degrade at 32K on NoLiMa ([primary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/adobe-research/NoLiMa/main/README.md)).
   - Routers recover most of the quality at much lower cost: Self-Route saves 39-65% of tokens ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.16833)); Hybrid LLM makes up to 40% fewer large-model calls ([primary](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/hybrid-llm-cost-efficient-and-quality-aware-query-routing/)); TF-IDF+SVM routing reaches 93.2% accuracy ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03455)).
   - Platform limits: PCC needs a network connection and has per-user daily limits ([primary](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2026/319/)).
5. **For whole-library questions, map-reduce with structured, confidence-tagged intermediate outputs, re-grounded in sources and pruned by a cheap relevance rater.**
   - Supporting evidence: Apple's recommended multi-session splitting ([primary](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3193-managing-the-on-device-foundation-model-s-context-window)); a 4B model beating 70B long-context models with this protocol ([primary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/thunlp/LLMxMapReduce/main/LLMxMapReduce_V1/README.md)); the 77% cost cut from a GPT-4o-mini rater ([primary](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/graphrag-improving-global-search-via-dynamic-community-selection/)); hallucination amplification in plain hierarchical merging ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00977)).
6. **Use the RAPTOR-lite summary layer for global questions only, with every summary node linked to its source chunks. Answer factoid lookups from chunks.**
   - Supporting evidence: structure-heavy retrieval costs 5-10 F1 on simple QA ([inferred](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/hipporag-2)); vector RAG remains strongest for local queries ([primary](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/blog/benchmarkqed-automated-benchmarking-of-rag-systems/)); graph structure pays only as a pointer back to source evidence ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18099)).
   - Small-model summarizers and extractors are fragile: MiniRAG ([primary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HKUDS/MiniRAG/main/README.md)); LightRAG's 30B-class extraction minimum ([primary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/HKUDS/LightRAG/main/README.md)); Phi-4-mini failing the GraphRAG pipeline ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20815)).
7. **If prompting cannot make the 3B model abstain, cite, or resist distractors, the evidence-backed lever is RAFT or Trust-Align style training via a LoRA adapter, at the cost of one roughly 160 MB adapter per system-model version.**
   - Supporting evidence: Trust-Align's statement that prompting fails to adapt models to RAG, and its measured gains ([primary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/declare-lab/trust-align/main/README.md)); RAFT ([inferred](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.10131)); the Ret-Robust LoRA in FlashRAG ([primary](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/RUC-NLPIR/FlashRAG/main/README.md)); Apple's adapter constraints ([primary](https://developer.apple.com/apple-intelligence/foundation-models-adapter/)).

### Inferences
- Latency arithmetic for multi-session chains, my estimate from the 2024 figures of 0.6 ms per prompt token and 30 tokens/s on an iPhone 15 Pro ([inferred](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/introducing-apple-foundation-models)); the iOS 27 model and newer devices will differ:
  - One full 4K session with a 3,500-token prompt costs about 2.1 s before the first token, plus about 6.7 s to generate 200 tokens.
  - A fixed chain of five such sessions is therefore about 45 s of model time before any fallback.
  - This is why adaptive depth, stopping early when the first pass is sufficient (Adaptive-RAG's 1.03 against 3.0 steps), matters on device.
- The "1 lb" failure is consistent with two evidence-backed gaps, not just a pattern bug: there is no sufficiency or answer-type gate on the non-model path, and the extractive shortcut is not tested on unanswerable queries. Whether the shortcut should exist at all is not something the literature addresses directly.
- None of the above establishes that the app's current modes perform worse than the alternatives on its own data. That needs the app's own evaluation with gold answers and unanswerable queries, not LLM-judge win rates (see the GraphRAG evaluation-bias finding in section 4).

### Gaps
- No published work evaluates any of these designs on Apple's on-device or PCC models, on personal-document libraries, or on phones with measured latency and energy for multi-session chains.
- Whether PCC may receive intermediate retrieval content (map outputs, candidate passages) under this app's consent design is a product and legal question, not a research one. Several implications (4, 5) depend on it.
