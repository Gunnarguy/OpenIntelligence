# Evaluation Process

This is the practical sales-process sheet for early conversations.
It is not legal advice.
Use it to stay consistent about when to use no paper, an NDA, or a fuller evaluation agreement.

## Default Rule

Do not force an NDA too early.

The safest founder path is:

1. Intro outreach
2. First call
3. High-level live demo
4. Confirm real technical interest
5. NDA if you are about to share non-public details or receive their sensitive materials
6. Evaluation or beta agreement before hands-on access to pre-release software
7. Paid evaluation or pilot agreement for real work

## When You Do Not Need An NDA Yet

No NDA is usually fine for:

- cold email or DM
- intro call
- high-level product positioning
- a live demo that only shows behavior
- sending the high-level one-pager

At this stage, keep the discussion at the capability level:

- what the engine does
- who it is for
- why the current offer is founder-led
- what the next step would look like

## When You Should Have An NDA Ready

Use an NDA before:

- sending deeper non-public technical materials
- sharing detailed architecture or packaging notes
- discussing non-public roadmap details
- receiving their internal documents, product plans, or customer materials
- sending the evaluation packet to a serious prospect when the discussion is moving beyond high-level founder qualification

## Which NDA To Use

Use a one-way NDA if:

- you are mainly disclosing
- they are mostly evaluating your material

Use a mutual NDA if:

- they will share internal files
- they will share roadmap or product details
- the conversation is clearly bilateral

## What The NDA Should Protect

At minimum, the NDA process should protect:

- confidential technical information
- non-public product and roadmap details
- sample data and internal files
- commercial terms that are not public

## Why An NDA Is Not Enough

An NDA only covers confidentiality.

It does not fully cover pre-release software access.

If someone is going to get the evaluation host, packet, or any hands-on pre-release engine access, you also want an evaluation or beta agreement that covers:

- limited evaluation-only license
- no production use
- no redistribution
- no reverse engineering or decompilation
- no public benchmarking or public statements without approval
- confidentiality
- feedback rights
- support boundaries
- warranty disclaimer
- liability cap
- term and termination

## Practical Sharing Rules

### Stage 1: Intro

Safe to share:

- one-pager
- pitch lines
- high-level demo description

Do not share:

- packet contents
- deep architecture notes
- internal docs

### Stage 2: Post-Interest, Pre-NDA

Safe to share:

- live demo
- simple API shape
- current commercial structure
- the fact that a buyer-safe evaluation packet exists

Do not share:

- detailed technical packet
- internal build notes
- partner-specific non-public commitments

### Stage 3: Post-NDA

Safe to share:

- evaluation packet
- deeper technical notes
- integration discussion
- their own sample materials if needed

### Stage 4: Hands-On Evaluation

Required:

- evaluation or beta agreement
- clear scope
- clear term
- clear success criteria

## Best Current Commercial Sequence

For OpenIntelligence right now, the strongest sequence is:

1. Send one-pager
2. Run founder call
3. Show live demo
4. If serious, execute NDA
5. If they want hands-on access, execute evaluation or beta terms
6. Convert into a paid evaluation or design-partner pilot

## Tomorrow-Safe Script

If Dave asks, "Do we need an NDA for every call?"

Use this:

"No. Not for cold outreach or a first intro. We want the NDA ready for serious prospects before we share non-public technical detail or let anyone into hands-on evaluation."

If Dave asks, "What do we need before software access?"

Use this:

"An NDA alone is not enough. Before real hands-on pre-release access, we want evaluation terms that cover license scope, confidentiality, no reverse engineering, no redistribution, and support boundaries."

## Internal Rule

Do not send the full evaluation packet casually.

Use the paper stack in order:

- one-pager first
- NDA second
- evaluation terms third
- paid pilot fourth
