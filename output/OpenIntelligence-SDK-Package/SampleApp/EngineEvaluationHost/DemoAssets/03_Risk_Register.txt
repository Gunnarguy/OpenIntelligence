OpenIntelligence Risk Register
Working draft for founder review

Risk 1: Evaluation packaging is still toolchain-sensitive
Severity: High
Probability: Medium
Why it matters:
The current evaluation SDK handoff works on the same Xcode and Swift generation it was built with, but it is not yet the final sealed binary distribution story. A buyer can evaluate the engine now, but broad self-serve distribution would still be risky.
Evidence:
- The repo currently relies on a staged evaluation XCFramework plus support artifacts.
- The original Engine build target is no longer the primary shared build path in the project.
- Module-stable packaging work remains on the roadmap.
Mitigation:
- Sell founder-led evaluations first.
- Keep the current promise narrow: same-toolchain evaluation, not universal SDK distribution.
- Continue tightening the final module-stable packaging path.

Risk 2: Security and procurement review may slow down enterprise deals
Severity: High
Probability: Medium
Why it matters:
The privacy-first story gets attention, but buyers still ask for clear answers on data handling, logging boundaries, deployment assumptions, and model execution posture.
Evidence:
- Enterprise IT teams said privacy alone is not enough; they want operational clarity.
- Healthcare and finance prospects both raised auditability and handling questions early.
- The strongest buyers are exactly the ones with longer review cycles.
Mitigation:
- Prepare a short technical one-pager on data boundaries.
- Keep the product claims precise and conservative.
- Use the evaluation host to demonstrate grounded behavior and explicit warnings.

Risk 3: Live demo quality depends on Apple Intelligence-capable hardware
Severity: Medium
Probability: High
Why it matters:
The strongest room demo currently runs on Apple Intelligence-capable hardware. That improves the story, but narrows the hardware surface for live evaluation.
Evidence:
- Simulator is useful for import and UI validation, but not the full Apple Intelligence runtime path.
- The most convincing experience is on-device ingest plus grounded query.
- Some prospects will not have matching hardware available during early evaluation.
Mitigation:
- Always bring a supported iPhone to live demos.
- Keep a clean simulator walkthrough as a fallback for UI and import proof.
- Continue improving the packaging and host app so the device path feels predictable.

Risk 4: Expectation mismatch between evaluation UI and final product
Severity: Medium
Probability: Medium
Why it matters:
The current host app is strong enough for a pitch, but it is still an evaluation environment rather than a polished end-user product. Buyers may project missing enterprise features onto the current demo.
Evidence:
- The strongest parts of the demo are ingest, query, and citations.
- Workflow automation, admin controls, and full product surfaces are not the present focus.
- Early buyers often assume the demo shell represents the shipped product unless told otherwise.
Mitigation:
- Frame the host app as an evaluation demo.
- Keep the commercial discussion focused on the engine capability and integration path.
- Use a crisp operator script so the room narrative stays disciplined.

Risk 5: Messy source documents can reduce trust if evidence quality drops
Severity: Medium
Probability: Medium
Why it matters:
Buyers will inevitably test scans, long PDFs, and noisy packets. If the answer is weak or the citation quality drops, trust can erode quickly.
Evidence:
- Buyers explicitly asked how the system behaves on messy PDFs.
- The entire product wedge depends on citation faithfulness.
- A single bad live answer can outweigh several good ones in an executive room.
Mitigation:
- Curate the live demo dataset carefully.
- Prefer high-signal document sets for first evaluations.
- Continue improving refusal behavior when the evidence is weak.
