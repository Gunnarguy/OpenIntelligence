OpenIntelligence Founder Briefing
April 20, 2026

Purpose
OpenIntelligence Engine is the private-document intelligence layer for Apple-native teams that need grounded answers from sensitive materials without sending those materials to a generic cloud AI stack first.

What we are selling first
- A founder-led design-partner evaluation, not a mass self-serve SDK launch.
- The product wedge is private document question answering with citations, fast enough for live internal workflows such as board prep, diligence review, customer research synthesis, and account-team briefing.
- The right first buyer is a team that already lives in the Apple ecosystem and cares about privacy, trust, and operator speed more than broad cross-platform coverage.

Traction snapshot
- We have 14 warm conversations in the current pipeline.
- 5 teams asked for a technical evaluation after seeing the private-doc grounded-answering flow.
- 3 verticals are showing the strongest pull: venture and finance operators, healthcare operations teams, and enterprise IT groups handling sensitive internal docs.
- The common ask is not "give us a chatbot." The ask is "give us trusted answers from our private material, and show the evidence."

What the product demo must prove
- Index a small document set in front of the buyer.
- Ask a real question in natural language.
- Return a concise answer with evidence attached.
- Make it obvious that the system knows when it is uncertain.
- Keep the story anchored on privacy and groundedness, not on generic model fireworks.

Commercial wedge
- The initial commercial motion is a 4-to-6 week design-partner evaluation with direct founder support.
- Target pricing for that first motion is 25 thousand to 40 thousand dollars depending on document volume, onboarding depth, and custom evaluation work.
- The near-term promise is faster decision prep and less manual document hunting for high-leverage teams.

Why buyers are leaning in
- Teams already have too many PDFs, research notes, diligence memos, and customer transcripts.
- Internal teams do not trust uncited answers.
- Security reviewers push back when sensitive material has to leave the device or move into an unscoped external system.
- Apple-native teams like the fact that the story can stay local, private, and operator friendly.

What is working
- The pitch demo can now run as a real SwiftUI host app.
- The evaluation flow supports ingest, grounded query, confidence, warnings, and citations.
- Buyers consistently react to the citation layer more strongly than to raw answer fluency.

Biggest operating risks
- Packaging risk: the current SDK handoff is evaluation-ready on a matching Xcode and Swift toolchain, but it is not yet the final toolchain-agnostic binary SDK.
- Security-review drag: enterprise buyers still want clear answers on data handling, logging, and boundary guarantees even when the positioning is privacy first.
- Onboarding friction: the strongest live experience currently depends on Apple Intelligence-capable hardware, which narrows who can see the full demo path immediately.

30-day priorities
- Convert one founder-led evaluation into a paid design-partner pilot.
- Tighten the packaging story so the buyer handoff feels lower-risk.
- Keep the demo centered on trust, citations, and speed to answer.
- Gather sharper proof points from real document sets, especially around time saved before high-stakes meetings.
