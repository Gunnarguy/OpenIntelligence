OpenIntelligence Design Partner Research
Condensed notes from six early buyer conversations

Recurring pain points
- Every team described document sprawl. Board decks, call notes, PDFs, research packets, compliance docs, and internal memos are scattered across too many folders and tools.
- Buyers said the hardest part is not generating language. It is finding the exact source fast enough to trust the answer before a meeting.
- Multiple operators said they are already overwhelmed by AI products that sound fluent but cannot show where the answer came from.

Signal from the calls
- Finance and investing teams want to ask questions across diligence packets, portfolio updates, and internal strategy memos.
- Healthcare operations teams want internal answers without sending sensitive material into a generic external workflow.
- Enterprise IT teams said policy, audit, and architecture docs are searchable today, but not explainable in a way leaders can trust under pressure.

Representative buyer statements
- "If the answer does not show me the document evidence, I still have to do the manual search."
- "Our problem is not writing. Our problem is verifying."
- "We have information everywhere, but nobody wants to be the person who repeats the wrong thing in a board meeting."
- "Privacy matters, but trust matters more. If it is private and still hallucinates, that is not useful."

What buyers care about most
- Citation-backed answers.
- Fast time to value with existing private documents.
- Clear handling of uncertainty and warnings.
- A setup path that does not require a giant backend project before evaluation.

What they do not want
- A novelty chatbot with no evidence.
- A long implementation cycle before seeing value.
- A demo that only works on perfectly formatted documents.
- A product story that hides behind "the model is smart" instead of proving retrieval and grounding.

Patterns across the interviews
- The most urgent workflows are meeting prep, investment or diligence review, executive briefing, and cross-functional handoff.
- Buyers want summaries, but the real economic value is reducing the time spent searching through scattered material.
- Several teams said they could justify a paid pilot if the product saved even one or two hours per high-stakes weekly meeting.

Common objections
- "How brittle is this when the PDFs are messy?"
- "Can the engine refuse to answer when the evidence is weak?"
- "What does deployment look like after the founder-led evaluation?"
- "How much support do we need from you to keep this running?"

Takeaway
The product wedge is not generic AI productivity. The wedge is trusted answers from private documents, with citations, in the moments where people cannot afford to be wrong.
